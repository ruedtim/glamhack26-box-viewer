What this is

Labels of dealers, auction houses, exhibitions and transport firms on the backs of artworks are traces of their history. This site shows where such labels, stamps and marks sit on the backs of works in the collection of the Stiftung für Kunst, Kultur und Geschichte (SKKG), Winterthur, and which works carry the same label. It was made at GLAMhack 2026 for challenge 304 «Provenance Pursuit».

How it was found

  1. Image index. All photographs of backs were cut into overlapping windows and described with DINOv2 (an image model), so that a photo of a label can be searched like a picture search.
  2. Image matching. The most similar windows are checked with SuperPoint + LightGlue + MAGSAC: the same physical label has dozens to hundreds of points that agree geometrically. This places the photos of labels and the pictures in SKKG's label register on the backs (as when locating aerial photographs on a map).
  3. Reading the text. The 236 provenance data sheets describe marks with their position («o. r. Zierrahmen» = top right of the frame) and text. Text recognition (OCR) on the backs finds many of them by their text, some only when the area the data sheet names is read on its own or after an image filter (flat field, single colour channels).
  4. Templates. Labels whose position is known serve as templates for the same label elsewhere.
  5. A vision-language model (Qwen3-VL, running locally) proposes a box for the remaining marks from the data sheet's description. These boxes are leads and marked as unverified.
  6. A label detector (OWLv2, an open-vocabulary object detector, running locally) searched all backs for anything that looks like a label or stamp, without knowing what to look for. It found that no record documents yet, of them with text that OCR could read. Their issuer is only a guess from that text, and finds without text are often not labels at all, so all of them are marked to check.
  7. Found again. Every reliably located label is searched on all other backs. A hit is backed when the records of both works explain it: a shared exhibition, the same seller to SKKG, or the provenance.
  8. Exploded back and timeline. Every work's dialog lifts its labels off the back and sets them beside a provenance timeline from SKKG's own records: the stations of its provenance in order (dated where SKKG records the sale or the dealer), its exhibitions and its acquisition by SKKG. A label is linked to an event when its text agrees with the record: the issuer and the lot, date or year of a sale, the venue and year of an exhibition, or the same label on another work that shares the event. of events are documented by a label ( strongly).

How to read it

  • Boxes are in pixels of the original image (x, y, width, height) on the SKKG IIIF server; nothing is copied. «Whole back» opens the full photograph with the box in a zoomable viewer.
  • check marks boxes that are not reliable: uncertain matches, text found elsewhere than expected, similar (not identical) labels, the vision-language model's proposals and the label detector's finds.
  • A label found on another work is a lead, not proof: it can be the same design used for many years, or SKKG's own loan documentation.
  • In the timeline, strong links (solid frame) rest on two features that agree, medium links on one distinctive feature, weak links (dashed) are ambiguous: several events fit, or OCR read a name only approximately. Each link says its basis. Private owners, heirs and sellers who are persons appear only by their role; dealers, auction houses and museums are named as SKKG records them. A label without an event may document a station or an exhibition that the records lack.

Data and downloads

  • All results as a workbook (Excel), with a guide on its first sheet.
  • Review page: check the vision-language model's proposals (correct, wrong, unsure) and download your decisions for the team.
  • Entry form for the columns F to L of SKKG's label register (the register is German, so its terms stay German, with English glosses): opens from every box and label, prefilled with what the data already say (marked), keeps your entries in this browser and downloads them as CSV for the team.
  • What SIK-ISEA adds: exhibitions and sales that SIK-ISEA's research portal records for SKKG works and SKKG's knowledge graph lacks, with the matching labels on the backs.
  • Search by photo: photograph a label and find the most similar backs. Runs in your browser (the photo is not uploaded; the image model, 174 MB, is downloaded once). Confirm hits or redraw their boxes and download your decisions for the team.
  • Box viewer for any IIIF image: viewer.html?iiif=…&xywh=x,y,w,h
