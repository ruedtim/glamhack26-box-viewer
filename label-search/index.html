Have I seen this label before?

Photograph or upload a label, stamp or inscription from the back of an artwork and see the 20 most similar backs in the SKKG collection. Your photo never leaves your browser; the first search downloads the image model (about 174 MB, once).

How it works and its limits

The image model runs on your device; nothing is uploaded. The page only downloads the model (about 174 MB, once, then cached) and the search index (about 8 MB). On a phone, make the first search on Wi-Fi.

Your image is compared with 143,891 image windows on 872 backs of works in the SKKG collection (Winterthur). It is padded to a square with its mean colour, scaled to 224 px and embedded with DINOv2-base (Xenova/dinov2-base, fp16 ONNX, via transformers.js on WebAssembly; WebGPU is not used because its half-precision arithmetic changes the vectors too much). The 1,536-value vector is projected to 256 dimensions (PCA) and compared with 1-bit codes of every window. A back scores as its best-matching window; the thumbnail shows that window.

Only the coarse search runs here. On 197 test photos the correct back is first in 37% of searches, in the top 5 in 48% and in the top 20 in 59%. The full pipeline re-checks candidates with LightGlue keypoint matching, which lifts the first-place rate to 56%; that step does not run in the browser. Treat the results as candidates to look at, not as identifications.

When you check the hits, your decisions stay in this browser: download them when you are done (or before you leave) and put the file into the team's folder; they are merged with everyone else's.
