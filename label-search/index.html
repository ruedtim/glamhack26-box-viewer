Have I seen this label before?

Photograph or upload a label, stamp or inscription from the back of an artwork. The page compares it with 143,963 image windows on the backs of 872 artworks of the SKKG collection (Winterthur) and shows the 20 most similar backs.

Your photo never leaves your browser. The image model runs on your device; nothing is uploaded. The page only downloads the model (about 174 MB, once, then cached) and the search index (about 8 MB).

Optional: drag a box around the label to search only that part. Tap without dragging to reset.

Help check the hits: mark a back as same label, not it or unsure, and use Redraw box to draw the exact box around the label on the back (also when the label sits elsewhere on that back). Your decisions stay in this browser: download them and put the file into the team's folder; they are merged with everyone else's.

Drag a box around the label. The dashed box is the current one.
How it works and its limits

The image is padded to a square with its mean colour, scaled to 224 px and embedded with DINOv2-base (Xenova/dinov2-base, fp16 ONNX, via transformers.js on WebAssembly; WebGPU is not used because its half-precision arithmetic changes the vectors too much). The 1,536-value vector is projected to 256 dimensions (PCA) and compared with 1-bit codes of every window. A back scores as its best-matching window; the thumbnail shows that window.

Only the coarse search runs here. On 197 test photos the correct back is first in 36% of searches, in the top 5 in 48% and in the top 20 in 58%. The full pipeline re-checks candidates with LightGlue keypoint matching, which lifts the first-place rate to 56%; that step does not run in the browser. Treat the results as candidates to look at, not as identifications.
